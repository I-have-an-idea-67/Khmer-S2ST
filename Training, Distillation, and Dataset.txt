Executive Architectural SynthesisThe KHMER-SOVEREIGN-S2ST v10.6 engine establishes a deterministic, full-duplex bidirectional (EN <-> KM) direct Speech-to-Speech Translation (S2ST) architecture constrained strictly to 241.637M active neural parameters under PROD_240M_CONFIG and 3.838M parameters under CI_MICRO_CONFIG. Operating within a deterministic turnaround law of $\text{Total\_Turnaround} \le 1.94\text{s} < T_{\text{lookahead}} (2.00\text{s})$, the system pairs a zero-allocation Rust phonological and transport edge (khmer-linguistics-rs, khmer-transport-rs, khmer-dsp-wasm) with an autograd-native PyTorch core comprising Causal3StemSeparator, VocalModeGate, StreamingEarCartridge, DisentangledEmotionEncoder, AcousticPointerCodeSwitchBridge, KhmerMouthCartridge, and VocosISTFTHead.While the v10.6 blueprint satisfies all structural, dimensional, and real-time systems invariants across Adversarial TDD Gates 1 through 6, transitioning the 241.637M parameter graph from random initialization to studio-grade cross-lingual voice transfer requires a specialized training, distillation, and dataset engineering regimen. English and Khmer exhibit sharply divergent typological structures, acoustic-phonetic inventories, and resource availability. English is prosodically stress-timed with head-initial prepositional phrases and pre-nominal modifiers, whereas Khmer is an analytic, head-final Within-Noun-Phrase (Noun + Adjective + Determiner) sesquisyllabic language featuring a two-register voice quality contrast (A-series modal/clear voice versus O-series breathy/chest voice) and intricate consonant clusters. Furthermore, publicly labeled Khmer speech corpora total less than 1,000 hours, necessitating synthetic cross-lingual self-play and multi-teacher representation distillation to prevent overfitting and semantic collapse.1. Simultaneous and Streaming S2ST Alignment and Reordering1.1 Mathematical Audit of Kyutai Hibiki and Hibiki-Zero Contextual AlignmentSimultaneous speech-to-speech translation confronts an inherent structural tension between latency and syntactic reordering: emitting target speech before sufficient source context arrives causes semantic hallucinations, whereas waiting for full utterance boundaries violates real-time conversational turnaround. In KHMER-SOVEREIGN-S2ST v10.6, InMemorySession.append_pcm_chunk enforces a hard active-clause ceiling of $1.60\text{s}$ (MAX_CLAUSE_SLICE_SEC = 1.6) via Trigger #2, splitting continuous speech at the lowest-RMS 20ms window within $[1.20\text{s}, 1.60\text{s}]$. When translating a continuous utterance sliced mid-phrase at $1.60\text{s}$, an unconditioned non-monotonic syntax bridge (KhmerMouthCartridge.reorder_attn) lacks visibility into the prior clause's syntactic state and risks premature emission of ungrounded target syllables at the right boundary of the slice.Kyutai's Hibiki (arXiv:2502.03382) resolves this causality bottleneck by formulating simultaneous translation over a multistream token grid operating at a constant frame rate $f_r = 12.5\text{ Hz}$. Rather than relying on brittle test-time wait-$k$ heuristics that preclude batched GPU execution, Hibiki embeds causal wait behavior directly into the training distribution via weakly-supervised contextual alignment and silence insertion, permitting standard temperature sampling at inference.Given a source transcript sequence $S = (S_1, \dots, S_n)$ with word-level end timestamps $(t^S_1, \dots, t^S_n)$ and a corresponding target transcript $T = (T_1, \dots, T_m)$ with word-level start timestamps $(t^T_1, \dots, t^T_m)$, Hibiki identifies the exact source word index $i$ at which each target word $T_j$ becomes semantically predictable. Leveraging an off-the-shelf sequence-to-sequence machine translation model—specifically Google's MADLAD-400—the alignment algorithm evaluates the conditional log-likelihood of target word $T_j$ given all preceding target words $T_{1 \dots j-1}$ and truncated source prefixes $S_{1 \dots i}$ for $i \in \{1, \dots, n\}$:$$\log(p_{j,i}) = \log P_{\text{MT}}(T_j \mid S_1, \dots, S_i, T_1, \dots, T_{j-1})$$As additional source words $S_i$ enter the conditioning prefix, $\log(p_{j,i})$ increases in expectation. At the exact index where the governing source word or syntactic head $S_i$ appears in the stream, $\log(p_{j,i})$ exhibits a sharp step-function jump. Hibiki defines the marginal information gain $\delta_{j,i}$ and the raw contextual alignment index $a_j^{\text{ctx}}$ via the maximal log-likelihood difference across all prefix truncations:$$\delta_{j,i} = \log(p_{j,i}) - \log(p_{j,i-1})$$$$a_j^{\text{ctx}} = \arg\max_{i \le n} \left[ \log(p_{j,i}) - \log(p_{j,i-1}) \right]$$Because raw single-word log-likelihood differences can exhibit local variance on functional words or long-distance syntactic inversions, Hibiki applies two post-processing stabilization filters to $a_j^{\text{ctx}}$ prior to constructing the target acoustic timeline. First, any alignment delay spike exceeding $25\%$ of the moving average delay over a sliding window of five consecutive target words is clamped to eliminate outlier stalls. Second, to absorb timestamp estimation errors and prevent premature target articulation, the target word emission timestamp $t^T_j$ is required to lag the aligned source word's completion timestamp $t^S_{a_j^{\text{ctx}}}$ by at least a safety buffer $\delta_{\text{ctx}} = 2.0\text{s}$ in long-form interpretation.When constructing simultaneous training trajectories from offline parallel audio, whenever a target word $T_j$ occurs earlier than its causal predictability deadline ($t^T_j < t^S_{a_j^{\text{ctx}}} + \delta_{\text{ctx}}$), Hibiki enforces causality through silence insertion or alignment-aware synthesis. Under the silence insertion protocol, natural silence frames of duration $\Delta t_j = \max(0, t^S_{a_j^{\text{ctx}}} + \delta_{\text{ctx}} - t^T_j)$ are spliced immediately prior to the acoustic onset of word $T_j$, shifting $T_j$ and all subsequent target words forward in time. Because post-hoc waveform splicing can introduce audible cuts when word timestamps are imprecise or when adjacent words are co-articulated without an intervening pause, Hibiki complements waveform splicing with an alignment-aware TTS generator. The alignment-aware TTS receives the target text stream pre-padded with explicit silence/wait control tokens at the exact frame offsets dictated by $a_j^{\text{ctx}}$, enabling the acoustic model to synthesize natural prosodic decelerations, phrase-final lengthening, and smooth breath pauses.In February 2026, Kyutai advanced this paradigm with Hibiki-Zero (arXiv:2602.11072), eliminating the dependence on word-level aligned synthetic data entirely. Hibiki-Zero initializes on coarse sentence-level aligned audio to acquire baseline translation and voice transfer capabilities, and subsequently applies Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) reinforcement learning. By optimizing a composite reward that linearly trades off sentence-level translation quality (BLEU / ASR-BLEU) against temporal delay (Average Lagging), Hibiki-Zero learns to accumulate just enough source context to resolve syntactic dependencies before speaking, outperforming both Seamless and original Hibiki across audio quality, speaker similarity, and naturalness.Simultaneous S2ST / Streaming TTS SystemCore Alignment & Streaming MechanismFrame Rate & BackboneVoice Transfer & Prosody ControlKey Limitation for KHMER-SOVEREIGN-S2STKyutai Hibiki (arXiv:2502.03382)Weakly-supervised MADLAD log-likelihood diff ($\delta_{j,i}$) + Silence Insertion / Alignment-Aware TTS$12.5\text{ Hz}$ Mimi RVQ; $2.2\text{B}$ Temporal + Depth Transformer ($1.8\text{B}$ Hibiki-M)Streaming Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) over speaker promptAutoregressive RVQ token stream incompatible with non-autoregressive 15-layer OT-CFM DiTKyutai Hibiki-Zero (arXiv:2602.11072)Coarse sentence alignment + GRPO Reinforcement Learning balancing BLEU vs. Avg. Inside KhmerMouthCartridge.predict_syllables_and_align, the non-monotonic syntax bridge executes cross-attention where queries pos_q attend solely over the current slice's semantic representations bus_cond of shape $(B, T_{\text{ear}}, 512)$, spanning $30\text{ to }40$ frames at the $25\text{ Hz}$ StreamingEarCartridge frame rate:$$\text{reordered\_bus}, \text{attn\_w} = \text{reorder\_attn}(Q=\text{pos\_q}, K=\text{bus\_cond}, V=\text{bus\_cond})$$When an English speaker utters a head-initial clause bisected by Trigger #2—for instance, Slice $k$ containing "The Ministry of Public Works announced a massive infrastructure..." ($1.52\text{s}$) followed by Slice $k+1$ containing "...project in Phnom Penh" ($0.95\text{s}$)—two structural failure modes arise in slice-isolated translation. First, in English, the pre-nominal modifiers "massive infrastructure" precede the head noun "project", which has not yet arrived in Slice $k$. Because Khmer syntax requires the head noun (គម្រោង, komroung) to precede its modifiers (ហេដ្ឋារចនាសម្ព័ន្ធដ៏ធំសម្បើម), forcing KhmerMouthCartridge to translate "massive infrastructure" at the end of Slice $k$ produces inverted, ungrammatical Khmer word order. Second, when "project in Phnom Penh" arrives in Slice $k+1$, reorder_attn no longer holds the key-value states of "massive infrastructure" from Slice $k$, permanently truncating the modifiers from the translated output.To eliminate cross-clause truncation while strictly preserving the $\le 1.94\text{s}$ turnaround law, the [230M, 250M] parameter budget, and all 6 TDD gate contracts, the architecture integrates a rolling past-clause KV-cache with Hibiki-style contextual predictability gating.Rolling Acoustic and Semantic KV-Cache in InMemorySessionInMemorySession is extended to maintain a rolling cross-clause memory tensor self.past_clause_kv: Optional[torch.Tensor] of shape $(B_{\text{spk}}, T_{\text{cache}}, 512)$ alongside self.past_gate_kv: Optional[torch.Tensor] of shape $(B_{\text{spk}}, T_{\text{cache}}, 1)$. Whenever reset_on_seek is invoked during user timeline scrubbing, both buffers are immediately zeroed so rewind epoch fencing remains uncorrupted. When Trigger #1 fires on a natural pause ($\ge 250\text{ms}$ trailing silence), the clause boundary coincides with a natural syntactic completion, and past_clause_kv is flushed after decoding. Conversely, when Trigger #2 fires on the $1.60\text{s}$ active-clause ceiling (trailing_pause_ms == 40.0), the trailing $T_{\text{cache}} = 20$ frames ($800\text{ms}$ at $25\text{ Hz}$) of bus_cond from Slice $k$ are detached and stored in session.past_clause_kv.Causal Prefix-Extended Cross-Attention in KhmerMouthCartridge.reorder_attnDuring Slice $k+1$, reorder_attn attends over the concatenated key-value memory combining the prior clause's cached tail and the current clause's active semantic bus:$$K_{\text{ext}} = V_{\text{ext}} = \text{Concat}\left[\text{past\_clause\_kv} + E_{\text{past}}, \; \text{bus\_cond}\right] \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times (T_{\text{cache}} + T_{\text{ear}}) \times 512}$$where $E_{\text{past}}$ is a parameter-free negative sinusoidal positional offset $(-800\text{ms} \dots -40\text{ms})$ that distinguishes cached prior-clause context from current-clause frames. Because nn.MultiheadAttention(512, 8) accepts arbitrary key/value sequence lengths $(T_{\text{cache}} + T_{\text{ear}})$ while projecting queries pos_q of length $L_{\text{syll}}$, zero additional parameters are added to KhmerMouthCartridge, preserving the exact 148.931M cartridge parameter count and 241.637M total engine count.Hibiki-Style Entropy and Log-Likelihood Margin Gating at Trigger #2 BoundariesTo prevent predict_syllables_and_align from prematurely emitting dangling modifiers at the right edge of a Trigger #2 slice (trailing_pause_ms == 40.0), the module evaluates the per-syllable slot-prediction uncertainty (Shannon entropy across the 7-slot classification heads) over the trailing syllable query positions $l \in [L_{\text{syll}} - 3, L_{\text{syll}}]$:$$\mathcal{H}_l = -\frac{1}{7} \sum_{s \in \text{Slots}} \sum_{v=1}^{V_s} P_s(v \mid \text{bus}_l) \log P_s(v \mid \text{bus}_l)$$When trained on Hibiki-aligned synthetic data—where uncommitted right-edge modifiers are supervised with the silent wait state (onset_id = 0, vowel_id = 0, coda_id = 0)—any tail syllable position $l$ whose grammatical realization depends on future source words occurring after the $1.60\text{s}$ cut exhibits elevated slot entropy ($\mathcal{H}_l > \tau_{\mathcal{H}} = 1.85\text{ nats}$). When this threshold is exceeded on a Trigger #2 slice, the uncommitted tail query representations are zero-masked to emit a natural hesitational breath pause of $80\text{ to }160\text{ms}$ at the end of Slice $k$, while the underlying semantic frames remain preserved in session.past_clause_kv. Upon the arrival of the head noun ("project") in Slice $k+1$, reorder_attn attends across both past_clause_kv and the new bus_cond to synthesize the complete, syntactically reordered Khmer noun phrase (គម្រោងហេដ្ឋារចនាសម្ព័ន្ធដ៏ធំសម្បើម) without semantic loss.Python# Drop-in extension for backend/nn_cartridges/mouth_cartridge.py
def predict_syllables_and_align_streaming(
    self,
    fused_semantic_bus: torch.Tensor,
    gate_g_t: torch.Tensor,
    r_sps: torch.Tensor,
    target_lang: LanguageCode,
    orig_duration_sec: float,
    target_frames: int,
    explicit_syll_batch: Optional[SyllableTensorBatch] = None,
    past_clause_kv: Optional[torch.Tensor] = None,
    past_gate_kv: Optional[torch.Tensor] = None,
    is_hard_ceiling_slice: bool = False,
    entropy_wait_threshold: float = 1.85,
) -> Tuple[torch.Tensor, SyllableTensorBatch, torch.Tensor, torch.Tensor]:
    B, T_cur, D = fused_semantic_bus.shape
    device = fused_semantic_bus.device
    lang_idx = torch.full((B,), LANG_TO_ID[target_lang], dtype=torch.long, device=device)
    bus_cond = fused_semantic_bus + self.target_lang_emb(lang_idx).unsqueeze(1)

    # 1. Prepend rolling past-clause KV cache with parameter-free negative sinusoidal offset
    if past_clause_kv is not None and past_clause_kv.shape[1] > 0:
        T_past = past_clause_kv.shape[1]
        past_pe = -self._build_sinusoidal_queries(B, T_past, device, bus_cond.dtype)
        kv_mem = torch.cat([past_clause_kv.to(bus_cond.dtype) + past_pe, bus_cond], dim=1)
        gate_mem = (
            torch.cat([past_gate_kv.to(gate_g_t.dtype), gate_g_t], dim=1)
            if past_gate_kv is not None
            else F.pad(gate_g_t, (0, 0, T_past, 0))
        )
    else:
        kv_mem = bus_cond
        gate_mem = gate_g_t

    # 2. Cache the trailing 20 frames (800ms) if sliced by Trigger #2 (hard 1.60s ceiling)
    if is_hard_ceiling_slice:
        keep_frames = min(20, T_cur)
        next_past_kv = bus_cond[:, -keep_frames:, :].detach()
        next_past_gate = gate_g_t[:, -keep_frames:, :].detach()
    else:
        next_past_kv = torch.zeros(B, 0, D, device=device, dtype=bus_cond.dtype)
        next_past_gate = torch.zeros(B, 0, 1, device=device, dtype=gate_g_t.dtype)

    # 3. Non-monotonic reordering over extended (Past + Current) KV memory
    mean_r_sps = float(r_sps.mean().item())
    gamma_contract = 0.78 if mean_r_sps > 5.5 else 1.00
    L_syll = max(1, int(round(orig_duration_sec * min(mean_r_sps, 6.5) * gamma_contract)))
    pos_q = self._build_sinusoidal_queries(B, L_syll, device, bus_cond.dtype)
    reordered_bus, attn_w = self.reorder_attn(query=pos_q, key=kv_mem, value=kv_mem, need_weights=True)
    bus_l = self.reorder_norm(pos_q + reordered_bus)
    gate_l = torch.bmm(attn_w, gate_mem)

    # 4. Hibiki-style predictability / entropy wait-gating on right boundary of Trigger #2 slices
    onset_logits = self.heads["onset"](bus_l)
    if is_hard_ceiling_slice and L_syll > 3 and explicit_syll_batch is None:
        tail_probs = F.softmax(onset_logits[:, -3:, :], dim=-1)
        tail_entropy = -(tail_probs * torch.log(tail_probs.clamp(min=1e-8))).sum(dim=-1)
        wait_mask = tail_entropy > entropy_wait_threshold
        bus_l[:, -3:, :] = torch.where(
            wait_mask.unsqueeze(-1), torch.zeros_like(bus_l[:, -3:, :]), bus_l[:, -3:, :]
        )

    cond, syll_batch = self.predict_syllables_and_align(
        fused_semantic_bus=bus_cond,
        gate_g_t=gate_g_t,
        r_sps=r_sps,
        target_lang=target_lang,
        orig_duration_sec=orig_duration_sec,
        target_frames=target_frames,
        explicit_syll_batch=explicit_syll_batch,
    )
    return cond, syll_batch, next_past_kv, next_past_gate

Synthetic Parallel S2ST Data Generation and Multi-Teacher Distillation2.1 SOTA Synthetic Parallel Data Generation Recipes (2025–2026 Audit)Direct speech-to-speech translation models require aligned source and target waveforms sharing identical speaker timbre, emotional prosody, and temporal pacing—a training modality that is virtually non-existent in natural recordings. An examination of the 2025–2026 foundation speech literature reveals four distinct synthetic data generation architectures that solve this scarcity.Kyutai's Hibiki (arXiv:2502.03382) constructed a 40,000-hour French-English parallel speech dataset alongside a publicly released 900-hour high-fidelity synthetic benchmark from monolingual speech corpora. In the Hibiki pipeline, source audio is transcribed with Whisper, translated into the target language via MADLAD-400, and aligned at the word level via the contextual log-likelihood jump $\delta_{j,i}$. The target waveform is then synthesized by a voice-cloning TTS conditioned on a $10\text{s}$ speaker reference prompt extracted from the source recording, with silence insertion enforcing causal word delays. To compress the $2.2\text{B}$ model into the on-device $1.8\text{B}$ Hibiki-M variant, Kyutai further applied soft logit distillation over the synthetic parallel trajectories prior to supervised fine-tuning.Meta's SeamlessM4T v2 and SeamlessExpressive scaled multimodal mining by adding 114,800 hours of automatically aligned speech-to-speech data across 76 languages in SeamlessAlign using SONAR cross-lingual embedding similarity search. To transfer vocal style, speech rate, and pauses in SeamlessExpressive, Meta combined commissioned bilingual voice-actor recordings with synthetic cross-lingual prosody transfer, supervising a non-autoregressive UnitY2 unit decoder with hierarchical upsampling over discrete acoustic units derived from a 4.5M-hour pre-trained w2v-BERT 2.0 speech encoder.In March 2026, Fish Audio S2 Pro (arXiv:2603.08823) demonstrated a 10-million-hour multilingual pre-training recipe spanning over 80 languages without phoneme annotation or language-specific G2P. S2 Pro utilizes a Dual-AR architecture comprising a 4B-parameter Slow AR transformer that predicts the primary semantic codebook along the time axis at $\sim 21\text{ Hz}$ and a 400M-parameter Fast AR transformer that generates the remaining 9 residual acoustic codebooks at each time step, decoded via an EVA-GAN generator at $44.1\text{ kHz}$. Crucially, S2 Pro's synthetic annotation pipeline labels utterances with 15,000+ free-form inline natural-language paralinguistic tags (such as [whisper in small voice], [professional broadcast tone], and [laughing]) and applies GRPO reinforcement learning post-training where the exact discriminative models used to filter the training data (WER, timbre similarity, acoustic preference) serve directly as the RL reward models, eliminating distribution shift between pre-training and alignment.Complementing these generative pipelines, the Qwen3-Audio / Qwen3-ASR ecosystem (Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-0.6B and seanghay/Qwen3-ASR-0.6B-Khmer, 2025–2026) pairs a high-resolution Conformer acoustic front-end with the Qwen3 multimodal LLM backbone through iterative pseudo-labeling. Fine-tuned on ~700 hours (384,000 clips) of Khmer speech from Digital-Divide-Data/khmer-speech-dataset, seanghay/Qwen3-ASR-0.6B-Khmer achieves a 1.96% in-domain Character Error Rate (CER) with a 49.2% exact transcript match rate, providing a high-accuracy transcription engine for automated Khmer data curation.2.2 Step-by-Step "Reverse Cross-Lingual Self-Play" Pipeline (Y_km -> Text_en -> X_en)A fundamental asymmetry governs English-Khmer synthetic data engineering: zero-shot voice-cloning TTS models (Fish Audio S2 Pro, CosyVoice 2, IndexTTS-2.5, F5-TTS) achieve near-human parity when synthesizing English speech—with S2 Pro reaching 0.99% WER on Seed-TTS English—whereas zero-shot TTS in Khmer suffers from A/O register confusion and sesquisyllabic cluster errors. Consequently, synthesizing Khmer audio from English corpora introduces severe acoustic degradation into the target training distribution.The optimal solution is Reverse Cross-Lingual Self-Play: harvesting authentic, wild Khmer audio Y_km as the ground-truth target waveform ($x_1$ for KhmerMouthCartridge OT-CFM) and working backward to synthesize speaker-matched, duration-matched, emotion-matched English audio X_en as the source input. Training a 64.46M bilingual speech encoder from scratch on limited parallel data risks weak cross-lingual semantic alignment. By adopting the Semantic Distillation paradigm validated in Mimi and Fish Audio S2 Pro (arXiv:2603.08823)—where an auxiliary prediction head regresses the 16th-layer activations of a frozen w2v-BERT 2.0 teacher—StreamingEarCartridge inherits the phonetic and semantic priors of multi-million-hour foundation encoders.An important architectural consideration when distilling from 2026 open-weight speech encoders is reconciling their divergent temporal frame rates and hidden dimensions with StreamingEarCartridge ($25\text{ Hz}$, $d = 512$). Meta's facebook/w2v-bert-2.0 (600M parameters, pre-trained on 4.5M hours across 143 languages including Khmer) operates at a $50\text{ Hz}$ frame rate ($20\text{ms}$ stride, $d = 1024$); aligning its 16th-layer contextual phonetic representations $H^{\text{w2v}}_{16} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 2T_{\text{ear}} \times 1024}$ to StreamingEarCartridge requires $2\times$ temporal average pooling (kernel_size=2, stride=2). Conversely, NVIDIA's nvidia/canary-1b-v2 (918M parameters, 42-layer FastConformer trained on 1.7M hours of multilingual ASR and AST data) and nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3 (600M parameters, 24-layer FastConformer trained on 660,000 hours, achieving RTFx > 3300) utilize FastConformer's aggressive $8\times$ convolutional subsampling, operating at a $12.5\text{ Hz}$ frame rate ($80\text{ms}$ stride, $d = 1024$). Similarly, Cohere Transcribe (2B Conformer-based speech recognition encoder) and seanghay/Qwen3-ASR-0.6B-Khmer (600M parameter Qwen3 Conformer-LLM fine-tuned on 700 hours of DDD-Cambodia Khmer speech) provide high-level lexical-semantic encoder states that must be temporally interpolated to $25\text{ Hz}$ via linear upsampling (F.interpolate(..., size=T_ear, mode="linear")).To maintain zero inference parameter overhead and preserve the exact 64.46M parameter budget of StreamingEarCartridge, linear projection heads $P_k: \mathbb{R}^{512} \to \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{teacher}}}$ are instantiated exclusively inside CartridgeTrainer (train_cartridge.py) and discarded upon checkpoint export. Mid-network student representations at ear.blocks[7] (Layer 8) regress the $2\times$-pooled 16th-layer activations of w2v-BERT 2.0 to anchor cross-lingual phonetic boundaries, while top-layer student representations at ear.bus_proj (Layer 16) regress Canary-1B-v2 / Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3 on English inputs and Qwen3-ASR-0.6B-Khmer on Khmer inputs via a combined Cosine + LayerNorm Smooth-$L_1$ objective:$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}} = \sum_{k \in \{\text{w2v}, \text{can}, \text{qwen}\}} \mathbb{I}_k \cdot \frac{1}{T_{\text{ear}}} \sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{ear}}} \left[ \left(1 - \cos\left(P_k(h^{\text{stu}}_t), \tilde{h}^{(k)}_t\right)\right) + 0.5 \cdot \text{SmoothL1}\left(\text{LN}(P_k(h^{\text{stu}}_t)), \text{LN}(\tilde{h}^{(k)}_t)\right) \right]$$Python# Multi-Teacher Distillation Module for backend/training/train_cartridge.py
class MultiTeacherEarDistiller(nn.Module):
    """
    Training-only wrapper that distills w2v-BERT 2.0 (50Hz, Layer 16),
    Canary-1B-v2 / Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3 (12.5Hz), and Qwen3-ASR-0.6B-Khmer
    into the 64.46M StreamingEarCartridge (25Hz) with zero inference parameter overhead.
    """
    def __init__(self, d_student: int = 512, d_teacher: int = 1024) -> None:
        super().__init__()
        self.proj_w2v_l8 = nn.Linear(d_student, d_teacher)
        self.proj_canary_l16 = nn.Linear(d_student, d_teacher)
        self.proj_qwen_km_l16 = nn.Linear(d_student, d_teacher)
        self.ln = nn.LayerNorm(d_teacher, elementwise_affine=False)

    @staticmethod
    def _align_temporal(teacher_feat: torch.Tensor, target_len: int) -> torch.Tensor:
        if teacher_feat.shape[1] == target_len:
            return teacher_feat
        return F.interpolate(
            teacher_feat.transpose(1, 2),
            size=target_len,
            mode="linear",
            align_corners=False,
        ).transpose(1, 2)

    def _pair_loss(self, stu_proj: torch.Tensor, tea_aligned: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
        cos_loss = 1.0 - F.cosine_similarity(stu_proj, tea_aligned, dim=-1).mean()
        l1_loss = F.smooth_l1_loss(self.ln(stu_proj), self.ln(tea_aligned))
        return cos_loss + 0.5 * l1_loss

    def forward(
        self,
        student_l8_hidden: torch.Tensor,       # (B, T_ear, 512) at 25Hz
        student_bus_out: torch.Tensor,         # (B, T_ear, 512) at 25Hz
        w2v_layer16_50hz: Optional[torch.Tensor] = None,
        canary_top_12_5hz: Optional[torch.Tensor] = None,
        qwen_km_top: Optional[torch.Tensor] = None,
    ) -> torch.Tensor:
        T_ear = student_bus_out.shape[1]
        loss = torch.tensor(0.0, device=student_bus_out.device, dtype=student_bus_out.dtype)

        if w2v_layer16_50hz is not None:
            w2v_25hz = self._align_temporal(w2v_layer16_50hz.detach(), T_ear)
            loss = loss + self._pair_loss(self.proj_w2v_l8(student_l8_hidden), w2v_25hz)

        if canary_top_12_5hz is not None:
            can_25hz = self._align_temporal(canary_top_12_5hz.detach(), T_ear)
            loss = loss + self._pair_loss(self.proj_canary_l16(student_bus_out), can_25hz)

        if qwen_km_top is not None:
            qwen_25hz = self._align_temporal(qwen_km_top.detach(), T_ear)
            loss = loss + self._pair_loss(self.proj_qwen_km_l16(student_bus_out), qwen_25hz)

        return loss

def integrate_shortcut_sway_ode(
    mouth: KhmerMouthCartridge,
    x_0: torch.Tensor,
    cond: torch.Tensor,
    emo_tokens: torch.Tensor,
    steps: int = 4,
    sway_coef: float = -0.8,
) -> torch.Tensor:
    B = x_0.shape[0]
    device, dtype = x_0.device, x_0.dtype
    u = torch.linspace(0.0, 1.0, steps + 1, device=device, dtype=torch.float32)
    t_schedule = u + sway_coef * (torch.cos(0.5 * math.pi * u) - 1.0 + u)
    t_schedule = t_schedule.clamp(0.0, 1.0).to(dtype)

    x = x_0
    for k in range(steps):
        t_cur = t_schedule[k]
        dt = t_schedule[k + 1] - t_cur
        t_batch = torch.full((B,), float(t_cur.item()), device=device, dtype=dtype)
        v = mouth.predict_velocity(x, t_batch, cond, emo_tokens)
        x = x + dt * v
    return x


def compute_shortcut_distillation_loss(
    mouth: KhmerMouthCartridge,
    x_1: torch.Tensor,
    cond: torch.Tensor,
    emo_tokens: torch.Tensor,
    sc_ratio: float = 0.25,
    sigma_min: float = 1e-4,
) -> torch.Tensor:
    B = x_1.shape[0]
    device, dtype = x_1.device, x_1.dtype
    x_0 = torch.randn_like(x_1)
    t = torch.rand(B, device=device, dtype=dtype)
    t_view = t.view(B, 1, 1)
    x_t = (1.0 - (1.0 - sigma_min) * t_view) * x_0 + t_view * x_1
    target_v = x_1 - (1.0 - sigma_min) * x_0

    # 1. Instantaneous OT-CFM velocity regression loss
    pred_v = mouth.predict_velocity(x_t, t, cond, emo_tokens)
    l_fm = F.mse_loss(pred_v, target_v)

    # 2. Shortcut Self-Consistency loss across binary step halving (dt -> 2*dt)
    B_sc = max(1, int(round(B * sc_ratio)))
    x_t_sc = x_t[:B_sc]
    cond_sc = cond[:B_sc]
    emo_sc = emo_tokens[:B_sc]
    dt_val = 0.125
    t_sc = torch.rand(B_sc, device=device, dtype=dtype) * (1.0 - 2.0 * dt_val)

    with torch.no_grad():
        v_step1 = mouth.predict_velocity(x_t_sc, t_sc, cond_sc, emo_sc)
        x_mid = x_t_sc + dt_val * v_step1
        v_step2 = mouth.predict_velocity(x_mid, t_sc + dt_val, cond_sc, emo_sc)
        v_sc_target = 0.5 * (v_step1 + v_step2)

    v_sc_pred = mouth.predict_velocity(x_t_sc, t_sc + 0.5 * dt_val, cond_sc, emo_sc)
    l_sc = F.mse_loss(v_sc_pred, v_sc_target)
    return l_fm + 0.5 * l_sc

Architectural Modernization Proposals for v10.5A comprehensive audit of the v10.6 implementation against 2025–2026 speech synthesis and translation breakthroughs reveals five high-leverage architectural modernizations that significantly improve streaming robustness, brand code-switching fidelity, and voice-cloning naturalness while strictly preserving the 241.637M parameter count ([230M, 250M]) and all 6 TDD gate contracts.First, StreamingEarCartridge (ear_cartridge.py) and KhmerMouthCartridge (mouth_cartridge.py) currently execute unmasked full-slice self-attention during training. Incorporating CosyVoice 2's 4-way dynamic chunk-aware attention masking (arXiv:2412.10117)—where each training step randomly applies one of four attention masks (non-causal full context, full-causal past-only, chunk-$M$ lookahead, or chunk-$2M$ lookahead)—acts as implicit in-place self-distillation, transferring representations from wider lookahead modes to causal streaming modes without adding a single parameter. This multi-mask regime eliminates acoustic boundary artifacts when InMemorySession dynamically switches between Trigger #1 ($1.20\text{s}\text{--}1.55\text{s}$) and Trigger #2 ($1.60\text{s}$) slice lengths.Second, in s2st_console.py (forward_s2st and compute_s2st_training_losses), self.pointer(bus, bus) currently passes bus—the top-layer semantic output of StreamingEarCartridge (blocks[15])—as both semantic_bus and source_acoustic_features. Because bus has been heavily abstracted toward cross-lingual semantics by Layer 16, it loses the raw English formant structure needed to copy foreign brand pronunciations ("Calvin Klein", "Versace"). Routing the intermediate activations of ear.blocks[5] (Layer 6, where fine-grained acoustic-phonetic features remain intact) as source_acoustic_features while passing bus as semantic_bus enables AcousticPointerCodeSwitchBridge to perform genuine acoustic pointer passthrough whenever gate_g_t -> 1.0, requiring zero changes to tensor shapes or parameter counts.Third, both Kyutai Hibiki (arXiv:2502.03382) and CosyVoice 2 (arXiv:2412.10117) establish that zero-shot voice transfer similarity (SIM-o) improves substantially under Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) over the speaker/emotion conditioning stream. Adopting Flow2GAN's spectral energy-adaptive loss scaling (ICLR 2026)—which weights per-frame flow-matching errors inversely by reference frame energy $w_t = (\|x_{1,t}\|_2 + \epsilon)^{-0.3}$—suppresses low-NFE background hiss in silent and presyllabic intervals.Fifth, following Hibiki-Zero (arXiv:2602.11072) and Fish Audio S2 Pro (arXiv:2603.08823), introducing a lightweight 5,000-step GRPO reinforcement learning post-training stage over the 7-slot classification heads (mouth.heads) and LearnedDurationAligner directly optimizes the non-differentiable downstream evaluation metrics ($R = 0.5 \cdot \text{ASR-chrF2++} + 0.3 \cdot \text{SIM-o} + 0.2 \cdot \text{UTMOS} - \lambda_{\text{lag}} \cdot \vert{}\text{drift\_ms}\vert{}$), aligning discrete phonological predictions with end-to-end listener preference.7.
